# Bakery Austin - Food Safety Plan and Standard Operating Procedures

Fictional document for demonstration: it describes no real business.

Version 4, approved by Evelyn Harper (owner) on 2026-08-03. Next review: August 2027, or sooner when the menu or the equipment changes. Every employee reads this plan during orientation, and a printed copy is kept in the office next to the kitchen.

## 1. Scope and operation

Bakery Austin is a bakery-café in Austin, Texas, open every day from 6:30 am to 3:00 pm. Bakers start at 5:00 am. We bake bread, pastries, cookies and cakes on site, and the café serves coffee, breakfast tacos, egg sandwiches, a breakfast plate, quiche, soups, chicken salad sandwiches and fruit cups. Fourteen employees work across the week.

The food establishment permit is posted on the wall behind the register, where customers can see it.

## 2. Person in charge and active managerial control

A certified food protection manager is the person in charge during all hours of operation. The weekly person-in-charge schedule and the managers' certificates are kept in the Staff Training and Certification Record.

The person in charge applies active managerial control on every shift:

- at opening, checks that each handwashing sink has soap, paper towels and warm water, and watches handwashing and glove use during the shift;
- checks every delivery at receiving and initials the receiving entry in the temperature log;
- checks final cooking temperatures, the cooling log, and hot and cold holding temperatures;
- checks the sanitizer concentration at the three-compartment sink and the dish machine readings;
- signs the daily logs at closing. The general manager reviews all logs every Monday.

## 3. Employee health

### 3.1 Reporting

Every food employee and conditional employee reads and signs the Employee Health Reporting Agreement before starting work. Employees must tell the person in charge right away if they have vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice, a sore throat with fever, or an infected cut or burn on the hands or arms. They must also report a diagnosis of norovirus, hepatitis A, Shigella, Shiga toxin-producing E. coli, typhoid fever (Salmonella Typhi) or nontyphoidal Salmonella, and exposure to one of these illnesses.

### 3.2 Exclusion and restriction

The person in charge excludes from the bakery any employee who has vomiting, diarrhea or jaundice, or who is diagnosed with one of the illnesses listed in 3.1, and records the exclusion in the illness log in the office. An employee with a sore throat and fever is restricted from working with exposed food, clean equipment, utensils and linens. The person in charge notifies the City Environmental Health Office right away when an employee is jaundiced or diagnosed with one of the illnesses listed in 3.1, and restricts an employee with an infected cut or burn that is not properly covered. An employee excluded for vomiting or diarrhea returns to work only after being free of symptoms for at least 24 hours. An employee excluded for a diagnosed illness returns only with written medical documentation and, where the Food Code requires it, the approval of the regulatory authority.

### 3.3 Written illness policy

Sections 3.1 and 3.2, together with the Employee Health Reporting Agreement, are the bakery's written employee illness policy: they list the symptoms and diagnoses employees must report, when an employee is excluded or restricted, and how an employee is reinstated.

## 4. Personal hygiene

### 4.1 Handwashing

Food employees wash their hands and exposed arms at a handwashing sink for at least 20 seconds: rinse under warm running water, apply soap, scrub for 10 to 15 seconds, rinse, and dry with a paper towel. They wash their hands before starting work, after using the toilet, after handling raw eggs or raw meat, after touching their hair, face or phone, after taking out the trash, before putting on gloves, and every time they change tasks.

### 4.2 No bare hand contact with ready-to-eat food

Staff do not touch ready-to-eat food with bare hands. Pastries, cookies and bread are picked up with deli tissue or tongs, sandwiches and tacos are assembled with single-use gloves, and fruit is cut with gloves on. Bare hands touch only dough and other food that is baked or cooked afterwards. The bakery has no approved bare hand contact plan, so this rule has no other exceptions.

### 4.3 Handwashing sinks

The kitchen and the front counter each have a handwashing sink with warm running water, soap, paper towels and a sign telling employees to wash their hands. The sinks are kept clear and are used only for handwashing, never for dumping coffee or rinsing utensils. The opening checklist confirms that both sinks are stocked.

## 5. Purchasing and receiving

All food comes from approved, licensed commercial suppliers on the approved supplier list: Caprock Mills (flour and dry goods), Pecan Flats Dairy Distributors (milk, cream, butter, cheese and eggs), Little Arroyo Produce (fruit and vegetables) and Ridgeline Provisions (meat and poultry). Food prepared in a private home is never accepted, sold or used as an ingredient.

Refrigerated TCS food (milk, cream, eggs, cheese, meat, poultry and cut fruit) is accepted only at 41°F (5°C) or below, and frozen food only when frozen solid. The person receiving checks the temperature with a probe thermometer and records it in the temperature log. A delivery above the limit is rejected, and the rejection is written on the invoice.

## 6. Storage and preparation

### 6.1 Separating raw and ready-to-eat food

In the walk-in cooler, ready-to-eat food (pastry cream, chicken salad, cut fruit, cheese and baked quiche) is stored on the top shelves, and raw shell eggs, raw sausage and raw chicken on the bottom shelf, in covered containers. Raw chicken and sausage are handled on the red cutting board at the cook station, never on the pastry table.

### 6.2 Thawing

Frozen croissant dough, frozen berries and frozen raw chicken are thawed in the walk-in cooler at 41°F (5°C) or below. Food is never thawed at room temperature. When time is short, sealed raw chicken may be thawed under cold running water at 70°F (21°C) or less.

### 6.3 Shellfish and fish

The bakery does not buy, store or serve shellstock, fish or any other seafood, so no shellstock tags or fish parasite destruction records are kept.

### 6.4 Chemicals

Cleaning chemicals and sanitizer are kept in the locked chemical cabinet in the dish area, below and away from food, utensils and packaging. Every working container and spray bottle is labeled with the common name of the chemical. Only the chemicals the bakery needs are kept on site, and pesticides are applied only by the pest control contractor.

## 7. Cooking

The cook checks every batch with a probe thermometer in the thickest part:

- eggs cracked to order for immediate service: 145°F (63°C) for 15 seconds, unless the customer orders them over easy (see section 14);
- scrambled eggs held for tacos, and quiche: 155°F (68°C) for 17 seconds, checked at the center of the quiche at the end of baking;
- breakfast sausage (ground pork): 155°F (68°C) for 17 seconds;
- chicken breast for chicken salad: 165°F (74°C), instantaneous.

## 8. Cooling

Cooked TCS food (soups, stock, cooked chicken, pastry cream and quiche) is cooled from 135°F (57°C) to 70°F (21°C) within 2 hours, and to 41°F (5°C) or below within a total of 6 hours. Soups and stock are cooled with ice wands and then divided into shallow pans, uncovered, in the walk-in cooler. Each batch is recorded in the cooling log with its start time and temperature and the checks at 2 and 6 hours. Food above 70°F at the 2-hour check is reheated to 165°F (74°C) and cooled again once, or discarded.

## 9. Reheating

Soups, stock and quiche that were cooked and cooled here are reheated for hot holding to 165°F (74°C) for 15 seconds, within 2 hours, on the range or in the oven. The soup well and the steam table are never used for reheating.

## 10. Hot and cold holding

Hot-held food (soups in the soup well, scrambled eggs and taco fillings in the steam table) is kept at 135°F (57°C) or above. Cold-held TCS food (walk-in cooler, pastry display case and the reach-in under the sandwich counter) is kept at 41°F (5°C) or below.

Temperatures are recorded in the temperature log: the walk-in cooler at opening and at 2:00 pm, the pastry display case at opening, and the soup well at 11:00 am and 1:30 pm. A reading above the limit is checked again within 30 minutes, and the corrective action is written in the log.

## 11. Date marking

Ready-to-eat TCS food made here and held in the refrigerator for more than 24 hours (chicken salad, pastry cream, cut fruit and quiche) is labeled with the product name, the preparation date and the discard date. The day of preparation counts as Day 1, and the food is used or discarded within 10 days.

## 12. Time as a public health control

Breakfast tacos in the counter basket are the only food held under time as a public health control. They are taken from hot holding at 135°F (57°C) or above, wrapped, and marked with a discard time 4 hours after they leave temperature control. Tacos not sold by the discard time are thrown away; they are never returned to hot holding or sold the next day. This written procedure is kept at the counter for the regulatory authority.

## 13. Food allergens

### 13.1 Allergen awareness

The person in charge and every employee know the nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame. They also know the symptoms of an allergic reaction: hives, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, wheezing or trouble breathing, and dizziness. Every employee completes food allergy awareness training at hire, recorded in the Staff Training and Certification Record.

### 13.2 Allergen information for customers

Every unpackaged item in the pastry case has a card naming the major food allergens it contains, for example: Pecan sticky bun - Contains: wheat, milk, eggs, pecans (tree nut). The menu board tells customers in writing to ask staff about allergens and that the full allergen chart for every item is kept at the register.

### 13.3 Labels on packaged food

Cookies, granola and bread packaged here for self-service are labeled with the product name, the ingredients in descending order of weight, the net quantity, the bakery's name and address, and a Contains statement naming the food source of each major food allergen, for example: Contains: wheat, milk, eggs, soy, almonds. Labels are printed from the recipe database, and a changed recipe gets a new label before the product is baked again.

### 13.4 Preventing cross-contact

Nut products are made on Mondays and Thursdays only, on the marked table and with their own tools. After a nut product, the table, mixer and tools are washed, rinsed and sanitized before any other product.

## 14. Consumer advisory

Eggs on the breakfast plate can be ordered over easy. The menu marks those items with an asterisk, and the footnote on the menu and the menu board reads: * Served undercooked or cooked to order. Consuming raw or undercooked meats, poultry, seafood, shellfish, or eggs may increase your risk of foodborne illness.

## 15. Thermometers

Each cook station has a probe thermometer accurate to ±2°F (±1°C), and every refrigerator has a hanging thermometer. Probe thermometers are checked every Monday in ice water (32°F, 0°C) and adjusted or replaced when they are off by more than 2°F, as the manufacturer instructs. The check is signed on the Monday closing checklist.

## 16. Training

Every new employee completes the bakery's food safety orientation (handwashing, glove use, allergens, temperatures and cleaning) in the first week, and holds a food handler certificate from an accredited course within 30 days of hire. Records are kept in the Staff Training and Certification Record.

## 17. Premises, water and sewage

Drinking water comes from the city public water system, and sewage goes to the city public sanitary sewer. The bakery has no private well or other non-public water supply, so no water sampling is required.

Floors, walls, ceilings, doors and equipment are kept in good repair. Damage is written on the maintenance board by the kitchen door and repaired by the building maintenance contractor; the general manager checks the board every Monday.
